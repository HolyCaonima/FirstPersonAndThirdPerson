# Stage 02 — Phenomenal Character as Perspective-Indexed Relation

Date: 2026-08-12

This note records a refinement of the Stage 01 framework. The central move is to distinguish **first-person structure itself** from the intelligent system's later conceptualization of qualia, and to test whether "what-it-is-like" may be a perspective-indexed relational quantity rather than an additional substance.

## 1. The key split

The previous stage separated intelligence from subjective experience. This stage adds a second separation:

\[
\text{first-person structure}
\neq
\text{the intelligent concept of qualia}.
\]

A system may instantiate an intrinsic perspective without possessing the intelligence required to represent, conceptualize, or report that fact.

This suggests at least three layers:

\[
\mathcal R
\longrightarrow
(\mathcal R,p)
\longrightarrow
\Phi_p
\longrightarrow
M_p(\Phi_p),
\]

where:

- \(\mathcal R\) is the unpointed relational structure;
- \(p\) is an intrinsically realized perspective index;
- \(\Phi_p\) is the family of relations that become defined only relative to that perspective;
- \(M_p(\Phi_p)\) is an intelligent self-model of those relations.

The report

> "I am experiencing X"

belongs to the final layer, not necessarily to the existence of first-person structure itself.

## 2. A stronger hypothesis about "what-it-is-like"

There are two non-exclusive possibilities.

### 2.1 Reification hypothesis

The idea that experience contains an additional, private, irreducible **qualia substance** may be a reification produced by intelligent self-modeling.

The underlying first-person structure may be real while the interpretation

\[
\text{"there exists an extra internal object }Q\text{"}
\]

is a conceptual artifact.

In this view, the illusion is not that first-person presentation exists. The possible illusion is the objectification of perspectival structure into a hidden inner substance.

### 2.2 Perspective-indexed relation hypothesis

A stronger and more constructive possibility is that "what-it-is-like" is not illusory at all, but is instead a family of relations that becomes well-defined only after intrinsic pointing.

The guiding analogy is distance.

Without a distinguished origin, the quantity

\[
d_p(x)=d(p,x)
\]

is not defined. Once \(p\) is fixed, it appears automatically as a legitimate relational quantity. No new physical substance has been created; a new relation has become definable.

Likewise, suppose an intrinsic perspective \(p\) induces a map

\[
\Phi_p:\mathcal R\to\mathcal Q_p.
\]

Then \(\Phi_p(x)\) can be read provisionally as

> how state \(x\) is presented relative to intrinsic perspective \(p\).

The proposal is:

\[
\boxed{\text{what-it-is-like may be a perspective-indexed relational quantity}}
\]

rather than an additional object or substance.

## 3. Phenomenal identity as relational position

On this hypothesis, a qualitative state is not identified by a hidden scalar essence such as "redness-in-itself". Its identity may instead be determined by its position in an intrinsic relational geometry.

For a phenomenal state \(q\), its identity could depend on its relations to:

- other discriminable phenomenal states;
- memory;
- attention;
- action dispositions;
- bodily states;
- valence;
- self-related structure;
- temporal and causal neighborhood.

Schematically,

\[
q
\sim
\operatorname{RelProfile}_p(q).
\]

Thus the question

> What is the intrinsic essence of red?

may be replaced by

> What is the relational identity of the red-state in the intrinsic perspectival geometry associated with \(p\)?

This remains a hypothesis, but it gives "phenomenal character" a candidate mathematical type.

## 4. First person may be more fundamental than phenomenal self-description

The minimal hierarchy now becomes:

\[
\boxed{\text{intrinsic perspective}}
\]

\[
\Downarrow
\]

\[
\boxed{\text{perspective-indexed phenomenal relations}}
\]

\[
\Downarrow
\]

\[
\boxed{\text{intelligent self-model / qualia concept}}
\]

This makes it possible for a low-intelligence system to instantiate \(\Phi_p\) without possessing \(M_p(\Phi_p)\).

A hypothetical stone, if it satisfied the yet-unknown conditions for intrinsic perspective, might therefore possess some minimal first-person relational structure while lacking any ability to form the thought:

> "I have subjective experience."

This is a logical possibility within the framework, not an empirical claim that ordinary stones are conscious.

## 5. Why third-person descriptions may systematically miss phenomenal relations

Let

\[
U:(\mathcal R,p)\to\mathcal R
\]

be the forgetful operation that removes the perspective index.

If a phenomenal quantity is genuinely \(p\)-indexed,

\[
\Phi_p(x),
\]

then after forgetting \(p\), the unindexed question

\[
\Phi(x)=?
\]

may simply be ill-typed.

This is structurally analogous to asking for "distance from here" after deleting the reference to "here".

The absence of \(\Phi_p\) from a perspective-neutral description would therefore not imply that a physical ingredient is missing. It may mean that the operation used to construct the third-person description has removed the index required for that relational quantity to be defined.

This gives a sharper candidate interpretation of part of the explanatory gap:

\[
\boxed{\text{the gap may arise from forgetting the index on which phenomenal relations depend}}
\]

rather than from the absence of a special phenomenal substance.

## 6. A revised form of the hard problem

The classical formulation asks roughly:

> Why does physical matter produce something-it-is-like?

The current framework replaces this with two narrower problems:

1. **Intrinsic pointing problem**

   What purely relational conditions make a perspective index \(p\) intrinsic to a system rather than externally assigned?

2. **Phenomenal geometry problem**

   Given an intrinsic perspective \(p\), what family of perspective-indexed relations \(\Phi_p\) is induced, and what invariants classify its phenomenological structure?

The traditional question of why matter "produces" a mysterious inner substance may then turn out to contain a category error.

## 7. Updated conjecture

Stage 01 proposed that phenomenology might be invariant under intrinsic relational isomorphism. This stage refines the conjecture:

\[
\boxed{
\text{first-person structure is fundamental; phenomenal character is relationally induced; qualia-as-substance may be reification}
}
\]

More formally, if two systems realize intrinsic perspectives \(p_A,p_B\) and there is an admissible structure-preserving equivalence

\[
F:(\mathcal R_A,p_A)\simeq(\mathcal R_B,p_B)
\]

that also preserves the induced phenomenal relational family,

\[
F(\Phi_{p_A})\simeq\Phi_{p_B},
\]

then the systems should be phenomenally equivalent up to the corresponding relational isomorphism.

This remains conjectural until "intrinsic", \(\Phi_p\), admissible equivalence, and phenomenal invariance are rigorously defined.

## 8. Immediate research consequences

The next formal work should test whether this analogy survives scrutiny rather than merely sounding intuitive.

In particular:

- define the type of a perspective index \(p\);
- distinguish extrinsic pointing from intrinsically implemented pointing;
- define what it means for a relational quantity to be perspective-indexed;
- characterize the forgetful map that removes indexical structure;
- determine which relations disappear, survive, or transform equivariantly under perspective forgetting/change;
- ask whether a phenomenal relational family can be generated from intrinsic pointing alone or whether an additional primitive is required;
- search for toy models in which a pointed structure induces nontrivial internal relational geometry;
- keep intelligence, self-modeling, reportability, and metacognition formally downstream from first-person structure.

## 9. Current condensed thesis

The present hypothesis can be compressed to:

> The most fundamental fact may not be the existence of a mysterious phenomenal object, but the existence of intrinsic first-person structure. Once an intrinsic perspective is present, new perspective-indexed relations may become defined in the same structural sense that distance-from-an-origin becomes defined after choosing an origin. "What-it-is-like" may belong to this relational family. A sufficiently intelligent system can then reify and model these relations as private qualia, but that self-description is downstream from subjectivity itself.

If this direction is correct, subjective experience is not primarily a special material product of brains. Brains would instead be physical realizations of a more general perspectival relational structure.
