# Stage 01 — Relational / Perspectival Meta-Structure

Date: 2026-08-12

This note records the current stage of the project before further formalization. It is intentionally written as a research snapshot rather than as a finished theory.

## 1. Starting intuition

Across algorithms, mathematics, physics, and consciousness research, the same structural motifs appear repeatedly:

- one problem admits multiple representations;
- changing representation changes what is local, global, simple, expensive, explicit, or implicit;
- transformations between representations preserve some structure and destroy or hide other structure;
- local/global, process/object, primal/dual, coordinate/gauge, observer-relative/observer-neutral, and time-domain/frequency-domain pairs repeatedly show similar skeletons.

The working suspicion is that these are not merely disconnected tricks. They may be instances of a more general meta-structure concerning **presentations, transformations, invariants, and perspectives**.

The goal is not to force all examples into one analogy. The goal is to search for a common relational language and then test whether the mappings actually preserve composition laws, invariants, and obstructions.

## 2. Structuralist move: eliminate substance-first ontology

Instead of taking objects as primitive entities that later acquire relations, take relational structure as primary.

A provisional slogan is:

> An object is not first present and then related; its identity is the structure that remains coherent across admissible relations and transformations.

At a formal level, replace the picture

\[
\text{objects} + \text{relations between objects}
\]

with a hierarchy

\[
\text{relations},\quad \text{relations between relations},\quad \text{higher relations},\ldots
\]

This naturally suggests categorical and higher-categorical language, but no commitment is yet made that category theory is the final formalism.

A useful research heuristic is Yoneda-like: characterize an object by its relational profile rather than by a hidden, relation-independent substance.

## 3. Presentations and invariants

Let \(X\) denote a representation-neutral placeholder for a structure and let

\[
R_i(X)
\]

denote one presentation of it. Transformations between presentations are

\[
T_{ij}:R_i(X)\to R_j(X).
\]

The central questions are:

1. What changes under \(T_{ij}\)?
2. What is invariant?
3. What becomes explicit or primitive?
4. What becomes distributed, hidden, or expensive to recover?
5. Which transformations are invertible?
6. Which are quotient/projection/coarse-graining operations?
7. Which preserve information but change computational accessibility?
8. Which require a perspective index rather than merely a basis/coordinate choice?

This distinction matters because information equivalence does not imply computational or structural equivalence. Fourier transformation is a canonical example: the same information can make convolution complicated in one presentation and pointwise multiplication simple in another.

A future classification should distinguish at least:

- reversible change of basis / representation;
- duality;
- coordinate or gauge transformation;
- quotient and coarse-graining;
- local restriction / gluing;
- perspective change;
- intrinsic vs extrinsic presentation.

## 4. First person and third person as structural properties

A key step is to stop treating first-person and third-person as two verbal descriptions of the same event and instead model them as different kinds of relational structure.

Let

\[
W
\]

be an unpointed relational world. Introduce a distinguished perspective index \(p\):

\[
(W,p).
\]

Once \(p\) is present, a family of indexical relations becomes definable:

\[
\operatorname{near}_p(x),\quad
\operatorname{reachable}_p(x),\quad
\operatorname{accessible}_p(x),\quad
\operatorname{self}_p,\quad
\operatorname{other}_p,
\]

and potentially temporal or causal relations defined relative to the history associated with \(p\).

The crucial point is that inserting \(p\) need not add a new physical substance. It changes the relational structure by making a center or origin available.

This suggests the basic contrast

\[
\boxed{\text{unpointed structure} \leftrightarrow \text{pointed structure}}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{\text{perspective-neutral} \leftrightarrow \text{perspective-indexed}}.
\]

### 4.1 Forgetful asymmetry

There is a natural forgetful operation

\[
U:(W,p)\to W
\]

which drops the distinguished point.

The reverse operation generally has no canonical inverse. Given \(W\), one may have many admissible points

\[
p_1,p_2,p_3,\ldots
\]

with no perspective-neutral invariant selecting which one is "here".

This gives a candidate structural source for part of the first-person / third-person asymmetry:

\[
\boxed{\text{first} \to \text{third may be forgetting; third} \to \text{first requires self-location}}.
\]

This is one reason the consciousness explanatory gap may partly resemble a quotient inversion problem: information intentionally removed in constructing a perspective-neutral description cannot in general be recovered by a canonical inverse.

## 5. Pointing is not yet consciousness

A critical distinction:

\[
\text{pointed structure} \neq \text{subjective experience}.
\]

An external mathematician can mark a node \(p\) in a graph. That is only **extrinsic pointing**.

A theory of subjectivity needs a criterion for **intrinsic pointing** or **intrinsic perspective**: the distinguished perspective must be generated and maintained by the system's own causal / informational organization rather than imposed by an outside description.

A minimal placeholder is

\[
(W,p,\mathcal A_p),
\]

where \(\mathcal A_p\) encodes accessibility, causal dependence, or internally realized information boundaries relative to \(p\).

A candidate intrinsic-perspective operator may be written

\[
\operatorname{Int}(W,p).
\]

The central unresolved question is:

> What purely relational conditions distinguish an externally selected local view from a perspective instantiated by the system itself?

This is currently the sharpest technical bottleneck.

## 6. Subjective experience as a relational class

The current hypothesis is not that pointing automatically proves phenomenology. The weaker and testable hypothesis is:

> Subjective experience, if it exists in this framework, should correspond to some class of relations or invariants that arise only for intrinsically realized perspectives.

Instead of searching for an extra substance called "experience", search for a family

\[
\mathcal I_{\mathrm{phen}}
\]

of **phenomenal invariants** associated with intrinsic perspective.

A possible long-term conjecture is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{phenomenology is invariant under intrinsic relational isomorphism}
}
\]

That is, if two physically different systems realize isomorphic intrinsic perspectival structures, then they instantiate the same phenomenological structure up to the relevant equivalence.

This is only a conjecture. It requires definitions of:

- intrinsic perspective;
- admissible relational isomorphism;
- phenomenal invariant;
- identity conditions across different physical realizations.

## 7. Intelligence and experience must be separated

A major conceptual result of this stage is that intelligence and subjective experience should be modeled as different axes.

**Intelligence** concerns capacities such as:

- computation;
- prediction;
- control;
- model building;
- self-modeling;
- metacognition;
- communication and report.

**Subjective experience**, in this framework, concerns whether a system realizes an intrinsic presentation / intrinsic perspective at all.

Therefore

\[
\boxed{\text{reportability} \neq \text{experience}}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{\text{intelligence} \neq \text{subjectivity}}.
\]

A hypothetical low-intelligence system could possess subjective experience without the capacity to conceptualize or report it. Conversely, a highly intelligent system could in principle perform sophisticated self-description without this fact alone establishing subjective experience.

This means that statements such as "a stone could have experience" should currently be understood only as a logical possibility allowed by the framework, not as an empirical claim that ordinary stones are conscious.

The future theory should therefore ask:

\[
\boxed{
\text{What is the minimal relational structure required for intrinsic presentation?}
}
\]

rather than using intelligence, behavior, language, or self-report as definitions of consciousness.

## 8. Why consciousness may be a basic mathematical problem

If first-person / third-person structure turns out to be universal for any system admitting intrinsic perspective, then the distinction is not fundamentally a neuroscience-specific phenomenon.

Neuroscience would answer:

> Which physical systems in our universe realize this structure, and how?

The more fundamental mathematical question would be:

> What kind of relational structure can instantiate an intrinsic perspective at all?

A possible hierarchy is therefore

\[
\text{perspectival meta-structure}
\]

\[
\Downarrow
\]

\[
\text{conditions for intrinsic presentation}
\]

\[
\Downarrow
\]

\[
\text{physical realizations}
\]

\[
\Downarrow
\]

\[
\text{neural / computational implementations}.
\]

Under this view, the first-person / third-person split is a property of the structural language itself, not a late biological add-on.

## 9. Relation to other recurring structures

The project should test whether the same meta-language can faithfully encode examples such as:

- Fourier / time-frequency dual presentation;
- process vs completed trajectory / block description;
- Lagrangian vs Hamiltonian formulations;
- primal / dual optimization;
- gauge-dependent vs gauge-invariant structure;
- coordinate / reference-frame changes;
- local / global descriptions and gluing obstructions;
- algorithmic state compression and sufficient-state representations;
- observer-neutral vs perspective-relative descriptions;
- first-person vs third-person structure.

These examples should **not** be declared equivalent in advance. The goal is to determine which belong to the same transformation class, which merely share partial motifs, and which require genuinely different primitives.

The strongest evidence for a real unification would be a general theorem in the meta-framework that reduces to known theorems or constraints in multiple domains.

## 10. Current minimal formal skeleton

A provisional research object is

\[
\mathfrak M=(\mathcal R,\mathcal T,\mathcal I,\mathcal P),
\]

where:

- \(\mathcal R\): a family of presentations / relational realizations;
- \(\mathcal T\): admissible transformations between them;
- \(\mathcal I\): invariants and obstructions under those transformations;
- \(\mathcal P\): perspectival structure, including criteria for intrinsic rather than externally imposed pointing.

For a world \(W\), one may also consider a groupoid-like object

\[
\mathcal G_W
\]

whose objects are admissible perspectives and whose morphisms are perspective transformations.

Third-person structure may correspond roughly to the perspective-neutral family / unpointed object; first-person structure to a pointed member

\[
(\mathcal G_W,p).
\]

Consciousness then becomes the additional problem of characterizing

\[
C_p(W)=\operatorname{Intrinsic}(\mathcal G_W,p).
\]

The unsolved word is still **Intrinsic**.

## 11. Falsification / discipline

The project risks overcompression: at a sufficiently abstract level, unrelated things can always be made to look similar.

Therefore every claimed unification must state explicitly:

- the domain and codomain of the mapping;
- what composition law is preserved;
- what invariants are preserved;
- what information is lost;
- whether the mapping is invertible;
- where the analogy fails;
- what new theorem, constraint, prediction, or classification follows.

If no nontrivial cross-domain consequences arise, the framework may be only a useful philosophical language rather than a new mathematical theory.

## 12. Current condensed thesis

The current research intuition can be compressed to the following:

> Reality may be understood not as a collection of substances but as relational structure. What appears as an "object" is a presentation within that structure. Multiple presentations can expose different primitives while preserving deeper invariants. A perspective is a distinguished, possibly internally realized position in the relational structure. Third-person description forgets or quotients the distinguished position; first-person structure retains it. Subjective experience, if structurally reducible at all, should be sought among the intrinsic relations or invariants generated by such internally realized perspectives rather than identified with intelligence, behavior, or reportability.

The next research task is to define the relational primitives precisely enough that known examples can be mapped into the framework and compared without relying on metaphor.
