# First Person and Third Person

A working research notebook on a possible meta-structural account of representation, perspective, invariance, and subjective experience.

The central suspicion is that many apparently unrelated phenomena in mathematics, algorithms, physics, and consciousness research repeatedly instantiate a common family of structures:

- one underlying relational object admits multiple presentations;
- transformations move between presentations;
- some structure is invariant under those transformations;
- different presentations make different primitives explicit or implicit;
- local/global, process/object, observer-relative/observer-neutral, and first-person/third-person distinctions may be special cases of this more general pattern.

The project is deliberately **not** yet a theory of consciousness. The immediate goal is to formalize the meta-language first: objects as relational profiles, relations between relations, classes of transformations, invariants, obstructions, and perspectival pointing. Consciousness is then treated as a test case: if first-person structure falls out naturally from this framework, the hard problem may be partly a problem of representation and perspectival structure rather than a search for a special physical substance.

## Current working hypothesis

Let an unpointed relational world be \(W\). Introducing a perspective index \(p\) yields a pointed structure

\[
(W,p).
\]

This does not necessarily add a new physical entity. Instead, it makes a new family of indexical relations well-defined: here/there, self/other, accessible/inaccessible, reachable/unreachable, before/after-relative-to-a-history, etc.

A forgetful operation

\[
U:(W,p)\to W
\]

removes the distinguished perspective. This suggests a structural asymmetry: first-person-to-third-person may be modeled as forgetting/quotienting indexical structure, while the inverse generally has no canonical choice of \(p\).

The unresolved step is the distinction between **extrinsic pointing** and **intrinsic perspective**. An external mathematician can mark a node \(p\) in a graph without creating experience. A theory of subjectivity therefore needs a purely relational criterion for when a perspective is instantiated by the system itself rather than imposed by an observer.

## Important separation

**Intelligence and subjective experience are separate axes.** Intelligence concerns computation, modeling, prediction, control, self-description, and reportability. Subjective experience, if this framework is on the right track, concerns intrinsic presentation or intrinsic perspectival structure. A system could in principle have experience without enough intelligence to model or report that experience; conversely, high intelligence alone does not establish experience.

## Status

Early research program / speculative formalization. The aim is to convert recurring structural intuitions into definitions, mappings, invariants, counterexamples, and eventually nontrivial theorems. Similarity alone is not evidence of identity; every proposed unification must specify what the mapping preserves, what it destroys, and where it fails.
